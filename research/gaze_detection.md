## Gaze detection using head tracking

Attention and gaze direction are closely related, but they are not the same.

Attention describes where we allocate our limited neural resources to selectively process behaviorally relevant information from the environment. This applies across different sensory modalities. 

Gaze, on the other hand, describes where an animal is directing its visual system.

Although gaze and attention are not necessarily the same, where an animal is looking can provide an important indication of what visual information it is attending to. 

This relationship is particularly evident in animals with foveate vision, but gaze direction can also provide valuable information in animals with compound eyes.

In my [trackball analysis guide](/research/trackball/), I explain how behavioral kinematic parameters can be extracted from a trackball setup. The same setup can also be used to track head orientation and, consequently, gaze direction. Tracking locomotion and gaze simultaneously is particularly useful, as it allows changes in gaze to be examined in relation to the animal’s ongoing movement.
